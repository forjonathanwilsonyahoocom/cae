## Purpose

Define the operational tiebreaker policy when core repository principles conflict—specifically the tension between **Verification Loops** (exhaustive confidence-building with falsification attempts) and **Fast Feedback Budgets** (bounded iteration, speed, and resource constraints). 

Without an explicit rule, conflict resolution defaults to ad-hoc negotiation or context exhaustion. This document codifies how a solo maintainer (and future multi-contributor systems) resolves deadlocks deterministically.

---

## The Core Conflict

* **The Verification Directive:** *"Don't stop until confidence crosses the consequence threshold, including at least one falsification attempt."* (Optimizes for correctness, safety, and permanence).
* **The Budget Directive:** *"Don't exceed token/time limits; avoid elaboration-only progress and infinite loops."* (Optimizes for velocity, resource scarcity, and momentum).

**The Failure Mode:** When an agent or maintainer faces an ambiguous, high-consequence claim under a tight budget, the system either:
1. Breaks the budget to chase infinite verification (Prompt Roulette / Token Burnout).
2. Abandons verification early and merges an unverified claim (False Confidence / Silent Drift).

---

## The Tiebreaker Hierarchy (Solo Maintainer Rule)

When `verification-loop` metrics and `token-budget` limits conflict on a given claim, execution is governed by a strict precedence ladder. **Higher precedence outranks lower precedence unconditionally.**

```text
1. Safety & Reversibility Barrier (Can this mistake be rolled back easily?)
       ↓ (If reversible)
2. Consequence Taxonomy (Is this a high- or low-consequence claim?)
       ↓ (If tied)
3. Default Action Protocol (Action-bias vs. Pause-bias)

```

### Rule 1: Reversibility Outranks Rigor

* **Principle:** If a change is fully reversible with zero external side effects (e.g., refactoring an internal helper function, adding a draft markdown file), **Budget wins**. Terminate the verification loop immediately, accept the lower confidence state, and commit.
* **If Irreversible** (e.g., modifying database migrations, publishing public APIs, executing financial transactions), **Verification wins**. The budget must be expanded or the task paused, regardless of time cost.

### Rule 2: Consequence-Gated Budget Extension

When a verification loop exhausts its token budget before meeting its consequence threshold:

* **Low-Consequence Claims:** Drop confidence bar. Mark artifact as `verified_partial: true` with recorded caveats. **Proceed.**
* **High-Consequence Claims:** The budget exhaustion event *must* trigger a state change. The system cannot silently lower the bar. It must either:
1. Request an explicit budget extension from the human.
2. Transition the claim to an **Intractable State** (requiring human-in-the-loop review).



### Rule 3: The Default-to-Draft Policy

When human judgment is absent and a deadlock occurs between deep verification and delivery speed:

* **Default Action:** Mark the artifact as `status: draft` or `status: experimental`.
* Never merge an unverified high-consequence claim into `main` as `status: stable`. Force it into an isolated branch or clearly labeled experimental folder.

## Multi-Contributor Extension (Future Placeholder)

When PCI scales beyond a solo maintainer to multi-agent or multi-human teams, the tiebreaker shifts from personal heuristic to programmatic protocol:

* **Dispute Resolution:** If Agent A claims verification is complete and Agent B claims budget is exhausted, neither can override the other.
* **Escalation Trigger:** The claim automatically routes to a **Third-Party Arbiter** (either a designated human lead or a higher-tier reasoning model constrained to security auditing) with a 24-hour SLA.

## Keep/Discard Criteria for this Document

* **KEEP if:** It defines an explicit, unambiguous tiebreaker order ("When A and B conflict, do X; if X fails, do Y") rather than suggesting "team consensus."
* **DISCARD if:** It relies on hand-wavy governance prose like "carefully balance speed and safety."
